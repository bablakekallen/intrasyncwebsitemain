Schnell Machinery Without Schnell Software: Connecting the Rebar Shop to the Rest of the Precast Plant

November 18, 2026 • 3 min read • By Zachary Frye

In many precast plants the reinforcement shop is its own island. It has its own machines, its own software, and its own list of what to make today. The steel usually arrives at the form on time. When it does not, the pour waits. This post looks at the software Schnell sells with its reinforcement machinery, and at what changes when the rebar shop runs on the same platform as the rest of the plant.

What Schnell Sells on the Software Side

Schnell Group builds rebar and mesh processing machinery. Its software company, Schnell Software, offers:

  • Graphico Pro: software for developing rebar production lists.
  • Optimo: optimization of production orders for cutting and shaping machines.
  • Grafo Boss: production supervision and real-time shop monitoring.

These tools are built around the rebar shop, and many of their users are rebar fabricators whose whole business is steel. A precast plant has a different problem: the steel is one input to a piece that also needs a form, a pour slot, an inspection, a yard location, and a truck.

The Machines Do Not Require the Software

What CastLogic Does

CastLogic replaces the software layer on Schnell reinforcement machinery. The hardware integrates into the CastLogic platform, so reinforcement is driven by the same element record as the pour.

What Changes When the Rebar Shop Joins the Platform

  • One source for the steel: reinforcement requirements come from the element record that also drives the quote and the BOM. Nobody re-enters a bar list.
  • One schedule: cage and mesh production is sequenced with the casting schedule, so steel is ready when the form is, and not three days before.
  • Status on the piece: the production screen for a piece shows whether its reinforcement is cut, bent, tied, and staged.
  • Inventory and cost: steel consumption lands on the job, and stock levels update without a separate count.

Where the Design Data Comes From

Reinforcement data can come from CastLogic Designer for underground and utility structures, or from Tekla Structures, Revit, ALLPLAN, and other design tools that integrate with CastLogic. Either way it arrives once.

Mixed Reinforcement Shops

Reinforcement shops are often the most mixed part of the plant: a bender from one maker, a mesh welder from another, a straightener from a third. CastLogic is hardware-agnostic. Equipment connects over OPC UA, Modbus, REST APIs, MQTT, and other industrial protocols, and reports into one system.

Questions to Ask

  • Where does the bar list come from today, and how many times is it typed?
  • Does the rebar shop schedule follow the casting schedule, or a separate plan?
  • Can the production supervisor see reinforcement status for a piece without walking to the shop?
  • Can the machines accept production data from another system?

Plants that want to keep their current rebar software can connect it. CastLogic integrates with any MES and any machinery, so replacing it is a choice you can make on your own timeline.
