Moving Off Titan 3000: A Step-by-Step Migration Guide for Precast Producers

September 21, 2026 • 9 min read • By Zachary Frye

Titan 3000 has been the system of record for a lot of precast plants for a long time. That longevity is exactly what makes leaving it feel risky: years of structures, catalogs, customers, jobs, and the books all live in one database. The good news is that a well-run migration moves that history with you. This guide walks through how to do it, step by step.

Step 1: Know What You Actually Have

Titan 3000 is a full ERP, not just a production tracker, so the first job is an honest inventory of what your plant uses it for. Most producers find the data falls into three groups:

  • Engineering and product data — structure IDs, product and pipe catalogs, BOMs, and components.
  • Commercial history — customers, historical jobs, quotes, and sales orders.
  • Financial data — chart of accounts, open AP and AR, purchase orders, and payroll setup.

Also write down the workarounds: the spreadsheets, the side databases, the reports someone rebuilds every month. Those tell you where the new system has to do better, not just the same.

Step 2: Get a Clean Copy of the Database

Work from a database backup, never the live system. A backup gives the migration team something they can read, map, and re-read as many times as needed without touching the plant's day-to-day operations. Take a fresh backup again near cutover so the final import reflects current balances and open jobs.

Step 3: Map, Don't Just Copy

A straight table-to-table copy carries over every old inconsistency. Instead, build explicit mappings: which legacy structure IDs become which items, how catalog fields line up, and how the old chart of accounts rolls up into a clean general ledger. Save those mappings as templates so every re-import is repeatable and auditable.

Structure IDs deserve special care. Your crews, customers, and drawings all refer to them. Preserving existing part numbers means nobody has to relearn the catalog on day one.

Rule of Thumb

Every mapping should be re-runnable. If fixing one field means redoing the whole migration by hand, the process is too fragile to trust at cutover.

Step 4: Audit Against the Source

Compare the imported data to the Titan 3000 backup before anyone depends on it: catalog counts, BOM totals, customer balances, and trial balance by account. Fix the mapping, re-import, and audit again. Two or three passes is normal, and far cheaper than finding a problem after go-live.

Step 5: Train by Department and Run in Parallel

Train each department on its own workflows: estimating, production scheduling, QC, yard and dispatch, and accounting. Then run both systems side by side for a defined window. Parallel testing is where you catch the process gaps that no data audit will find.

Step 6: Cut Over on a Clean Boundary

Pick a cutover date on a natural boundary — ideally a month-end close. Take the final backup, run the final import of open AP, AR, POs, and in-flight jobs, reconcile, and go live with support on hand. Keep read-only access to the old system for reference until you're confident nothing was missed.

How Long Should It Take?

For a CastLogic implementation, a typical timeline is 4 to 6 months depending on the number of plants, product mix, and whether accounting is moving too. Rushing the mapping and audit steps is the most common way migrations go wrong.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I migrate my data out of Titan 3000?

Yes. A migration works from a Titan 3000 database backup and can include structure IDs, product and pipe catalogs, BOMs, customers, historical jobs, sales orders, and financial data such as the chart of accounts and open AP/AR.

How long does a Titan 3000 migration take?

For a CastLogic implementation, a typical timeline is 4 to 6 months depending on the number of plants, product mix, and whether accounting data is moving too. That includes mapping, auditing, training, and a parallel run.

Do I have to shut down production to switch off Titan 3000?

No. The migration works from database backups, so the plant keeps running on Titan 3000 while data is mapped and audited. Both systems run in parallel before a planned cutover, usually at a month-end.

Will my existing structure IDs and part numbers still work?

They should. A good migration maps legacy structure IDs directly into the new system so crews, customers, and existing drawings can keep using the same numbers.
