Moving From Concrete Vision to a New Precast ERP: A Practical Plan

November 25, 2026 • 5 min read • By Zachary Frye

Changing the system a precast plant runs on is a real project, and the plants that do it well treat it as one. If you are on Concrete Vision and planning a move, most of the work is deciding what comes with you, what stays connected, and when the switch happens. Here is a practical order to do it in.

Step 1: List What You Actually Use

Concrete Vision covers a lot of ground: contacts, quoting, engineering data, scheduling, project management, QC, safety, yard, dispatch, and field. Few plants use all of it the same way. Write down which modules each department opens every day, which custom fields and custom reports people depend on, and which were set up years ago and forgotten. The new system should be sized to how you work now.

Step 2: Map the Systems Around It

Concrete Vision's site lists integrations with Tekla Structures, Revit, ALLPLAN, Stack-It, ForneyVault, Idencia OneSource, and Constant Contact. Its module list does not include accounting, so your books are almost certainly in a separate system. Draw the whole picture before you move one piece of it:

  • Detailing tools: Tekla Structures, Revit, ALLPLAN, and AutoCAD integrate with CastLogic today, so these stay as they are.
  • Stack-It: structure design moves into CastLogic Designer, which is included with Production.
  • QC tools: inspections and quality records move into CastLogic Verify, also included.
  • Accounting: decide early. Keep your current accounting system and integrate it, or move financials into CastLogic Accounting so production and the general ledger share one set of data.

Step 3: Get a Full Copy of Your Data

Concrete Vision's FAQ states that its data is stored in a Microsoft SQL Server database. That is good news for a migration: SQL Server is a standard format that can be read and mapped completely. Establish who holds the database and how you obtain a full copy, and get that in writing before you set a date.

Step 4: Decide What Comes Across

  • Always: customers and contacts, product and piece definitions, open quotes, active jobs with their pieces and statuses, yard inventory, and open loads.
  • Usually: closed jobs for the past several years, NCR and inspection history, and bill of material data.
  • Rarely worth it: abandoned quotes, duplicate contacts, and fields nobody has filled in for years.

Migration is the one chance you get to clean house. Take it.

Step 5: Check the Result Against the Source

Before anyone trusts the new system, count. Active jobs, pieces per job, pieces in the yard, and open loads should match between the old system and the new one. Spot-check a few jobs end to end with the people who run them.

Step 6: Train by Department and Run in Parallel

Estimators, schedulers, QC, yard, dispatch, and the office each need to see their own day in the new system. A short parallel period, with both systems live on real jobs, catches the gaps that testing misses.

Step 7: Cut Over on a Clean Boundary

Pick a boundary that makes sense for both production and the books, such as a month-end. New jobs start in the new system from that date. If accounting is moving too, a month-end or year-end cutover keeps the financials clean.

How Long Should It Take?

What to Plan For

Plan on six months for a CastLogic implementation, from data preparation through go-live. Implementation is included, and a dedicated project manager runs it with you.

The plant keeps producing throughout. The old system stays live until the new one has proven itself on real jobs.

What You Gain on the Other Side

  • Operations and accounting on one platform, if you choose to bring financials across.
  • Structure design and quality control included, without separate products for each.
  • Scheduling that ISA can propose for your planner to review.
  • Unlimited seats, so the yard, the field, and the office are all in the system.
