Running Progress Group Machinery Without ebos: Your Software Options

November 16, 2026 • 4 min read • By Zachary Frye

Progress Group is one of the best-known names in precast machinery: pallet circulation plants, reinforcement lines, concrete distribution. It also sells a software stack to go with them. If you run Progress Group equipment, or are about to buy it, you have more software options than the quote may suggest.

What Progress Group Sells on the Software Side

Progress Group's software arm, Progress Software Development, offers several products:

  • ebos: the manufacturing execution system (MES) for precast plants.
  • erpbos: an ERP for precast producers.
  • stabos: collection and analysis of machine and production data.
  • Ahead APS: advanced planning and scheduling.

Together they cover the line, the business, the machine data, and the plan. They are separate products, and each is one more system to license, connect, and keep in step with the others.

The Machinery Does Not Require the Software

This is the part many plants do not hear during the equipment sale. The machinery is hardware. It needs production data and it reports status. It does not need that data to come from its maker's software.

What CastLogic Does

CastLogic replaces the software layer on Progress Group machinery. The hardware integrates into the CastLogic platform, which plans the work, releases it to the line, and records production.

How the Roles Map Across

  • Line execution (the ebos role): CastLogic Flow releases work to the line and tracks every piece through production.
  • Business system (the erpbos role): CastLogic Production and CastLogic Accounting cover estimating, scheduling, QC, yard, dispatch, and the books.
  • Machine data (the stabos role): line status, cycle times, and downtime report into CastLogic Insights.
  • Planning (the Ahead APS role): CastLogic Schedule plans beds, pallets, and crews, by hand or with ISA proposing the plan around your plant's limits.

The difference is that these are not four products. They are one platform with one record per piece, so the plan, the line, and the invoice cannot drift apart.

Mixed Floors Are the Normal Case

Few plants run a single maker's equipment end to end. A carousel from one supplier sits next to a batch plant from another and a reinforcement line from a third. A vendor MES is strongest on its own maker's machines. CastLogic is hardware-agnostic: equipment connects over OPC UA, Modbus, REST APIs, MQTT, and other industrial protocols, and everything reports into the same system.

Design Data Without the Relay Race

In a vendor stack, the model leaves CAD as an export, is converted for the MES, and is entered again in the ERP. In CastLogic, element data from Designer or from Tekla Structures, Revit, ALLPLAN, or AutoCAD arrives once and is used by every step after it.

Questions to Ask Before You Sign for the Software

  • Is the software priced and contracted separately from the machinery?
  • How many separate products will the plant run, and who keeps them in sync?
  • How is it licensed: per user, per station, or per plant?
  • What happens to the manual and wet-cast side of the operation that the MES does not cover?
  • Can the machinery accept production data from another system?

If you already run ebos and want to keep it for now, that works too. CastLogic integrates with any MES, so you can connect first and replace later.
