ERP for Canadian Precast Plants: GST/HST, CAD, Metric Units, and Bilingual Documentation

July 10, 2026 • 9 min read • By Zachary Frye

Canadian precast producers evaluating ERP software run into a familiar pattern: the product demos beautifully, and then the details surface. Taxes need a workaround. The strength fields want psi. The invoice template hard-codes a US address block. None of these is fatal on its own — but a plant runs on thousands of these details a day, and "the workaround" becomes how your team spends its time. Here's what Canadian producers should actually demand, and why purpose-built beats retrofitted.

Taxes: GST, HST, PST — Correct by Jurisdiction

Canadian sales tax isn't one number. Federal GST applies everywhere; the harmonized provinces roll it into HST at their own rates; British Columbia, Saskatchewan, and Manitoba layer separate provincial sales taxes with their own rules; Quebec administers QST. A producer quoting a job in Ontario, shipping pieces into Manitoba, and invoicing a Quebec GC needs the right treatment on each document — automatically, not from a lookup card taped to the estimator's monitor.

This is the first filter for any ERP candidate: create a quote and an invoice for customers in three provinces and check the tax lines. If the answer involves "custom tax codes you'll maintain yourself," you've found your future workaround.

Metric-Native Production Data

Canadian plants think in metric: element dimensions in millimetres, concrete in cubic metres, strength in megapascals, curing temperatures in Celsius. An ERP that stores imperial and displays converted metric will betray you in the details — rounding drift on cumulative quantities, batch reports that don't match the batching computer, strength specs that read 34.47 MPa because somebody's 5,000 psi got converted instead of specified.

Metric should be native, and imperial should be available alongside it — because Ontario and BC producers sell into the US, and cross-border work means drawings and specs in both systems. The right model is one record, both presentations, no manual conversion step anywhere a human can fumble it.

The Detail Test Beats the Demo

Any ERP can look right in a demo. Ask instead: enter a 35 MPa mix, a 2,400 mm panel, and an invoice to a customer in a different province — then look at every document those touch. Systems retrofitted for Canada leak imperial defaults and tax workarounds at exactly these seams.

CSA-Aligned Quality Control

Canadian plant certification runs on CSA A23.4 and the CSA materials and testing standards — not the US frameworks most ERP quality modules were templated on. QC checklists, test frequencies, and record formats should align with what a Canadian auditor samples, out of the box. CastLogic ships CSA-aligned QC checklists for Canadian producers, with records tied to pieces and pours so the audit trail is the data model, not a report bolted onto it.

Bilingual Client Documentation

Producers serving Quebec, New Brunswick, or federal work know that French-language documentation isn't a nicety — it's often expected or required. Client-facing documents should be producible in English and French without a parallel template set someone maintains by hand. If your market touches francophone Canada, put this on the requirements list where vendors can't wave at it.

The Rest of the Canadian Checklist

  • CAD as a first-class currency — including USD alongside for cross-border producers, with clean handling of exchange on quotes vs. invoices
  • Payroll-platform integration — connecting to the payroll systems Canadian plants actually run, rather than pretending to be one
  • Winter-aware scheduling — curing time and temperature tracking that reflects how Canadian plants actually run from November to March
  • Unlimited seats — plant floor, QC, dispatch, and office all in one system without per-user licence math working against adoption

Why This Usually Means Purpose-Built

Generic ERPs can be localized for Canada — the big platforms all have Canadian tax packs. What they can't be easily made to understand is precast: pieces with dimensions and marks, pours and beds, release strengths, yard locations, and QC records that trace by piece. Canadian producers evaluating software are really screening on two axes at once: does it know Canada, and does it know precast? Products fail the evaluation on either axis; the workarounds just look different.

Conclusion

The Canadian requirements — GST/HST done right, metric-native data, CSA-aligned QC, bilingual documents, CAD invoicing — aren't exotic. They're the baseline of operating here, and an ERP that treats them as configuration afterthoughts will tax your team a little every day, forever. Put the details in the evaluation up front, test them with real numbers, and choose the system that was built knowing your plant exists.
