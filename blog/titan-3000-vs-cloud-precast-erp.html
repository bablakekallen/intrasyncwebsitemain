Titan 3000 vs. a Modern Cloud Precast ERP: What Changes When You Switch

September 25, 2026 • 7 min read • By Zachary Frye

If you run Titan 3000 today, you already know the value of an integrated precast ERP: production and the business side in one system. So the real question isn't "integrated or not." It's whether a newer generation of integrated platform is worth the move. Here's a fair look at what changes — and what doesn't.

What Stays the Same

Both Titan 3000 and CastLogic are precast-specific ERPs that cover production and accounting. You aren't trading an integrated system for a patchwork of point tools, and your team's mental model — structures, jobs, schedules, shipments, invoices — carries over.

What Changes

  • Where it runs. CastLogic is cloud-native: browser and mobile, one live dataset across the office, plant floor, yard, and multiple plants.
  • Who can use it. CastLogic is licensed with unlimited seats, so supervisors, QC techs, and yard crews can work in the system directly.
  • Design to production. The CastLogic Designer configures structures and generates drawings, and that same record drives the quote, the schedule, and production.
  • What's built in. AI-assisted scheduling, machine-vision QC, ML drawing extraction from PDFs, and Revit/AutoCAD import are part of the platform.
  • Integrations. Open APIs connect payroll (Paylocity, Rippling), time clocks, bank feeds, and corporate ledgers.

Questions to Ask Before You Decide

Whether you stay or switch, these questions will tell you a lot — ask them of your current vendor and of any replacement:

  • Where is our data hosted, and who maintains the server?
  • How is the system licensed, and what does adding a user cost?
  • How often are updates released, and how are they installed?
  • Can crews on the plant floor and in the yard use it from a phone or tablet?
  • Can we import structures from Revit or AutoCAD, or extract them from PDF drawings?
  • If we ever leave, how do we get our data out — including accounting?

The Honest Answer

If Titan 3000 fits how your plant works and the answers above satisfy you, staying put is a legitimate choice. Switching makes sense when access, licensing, or missing capabilities are costing you more than a well-run migration would.

What the Switch Involves

Moving off Titan 3000 means mapping your structures, catalogs, jobs, and financials into the new system from a database backup, auditing the result, training each department, running in parallel, and cutting over at a month-end. A typical CastLogic implementation takes 4 to 6 months. We cover the process in detail in our step-by-step migration guide.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Titan 3000 a full ERP?

Yes. Titan 3000 is a full ERP used by precast producers that covers production and business operations, including accounting — not just batching or production tracking.

What is the difference between Titan 3000 and CastLogic?

Both are integrated precast ERPs. CastLogic is a cloud-native platform with unlimited-seat licensing, a structure Designer tied to production, AI-assisted scheduling, machine-vision QC, and open-API integrations, plus a dedicated migration path for Titan 3000 data.

Should I replace Titan 3000?

It depends on your plant. If Titan 3000 meets your needs, staying is reasonable. Replacing it makes sense when hosting, licensing, mobile access, or missing capabilities cost more than a well-planned migration would.

Is there a migration path from Titan 3000 to CastLogic?

Yes. IntraSync reads Titan 3000 database backups and maps structure IDs, catalogs, BOMs, jobs, sales orders, the chart of accounts, and open AP/AR into CastLogic with auditable, re-runnable mappings.
