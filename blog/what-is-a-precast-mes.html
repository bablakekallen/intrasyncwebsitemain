What Is a Precast MES? And Do You Need the One That Came With Your Machinery?

November 12, 2026 • 4 min read • By Zachary Frye

Ask three precast plants what their MES is and you may get three different answers. For one it is the screen at the pallet circulation line. For another it is the planning tool the machinery supplier installed. For a third it is a spreadsheet and a whiteboard. This post explains what a precast MES is, where it sits next to ERP and CAD, and why the one that arrived with your equipment is not the only one that can run it.

What a Precast MES Does

MES stands for manufacturing execution system. In a precast plant it is the software layer between the office and the production line. It has four jobs:

  • Take in element data from design: geometry, reinforcement, embeds, mix, and finish.
  • Plan the work: which element goes on which bed, pallet, or form, in what order, on which shift.
  • Send work to the line: release jobs to machines and crews so the right piece is made at the right station.
  • Record what happened: what was cast, when, by which station, and whether it passed inspection.

On a manual line those jobs are done by people with tickets and tablets. On an automated line they are done partly by machines, which is why machinery makers got into the software business.

MES vs ERP vs CAD

The three are often sold as separate products, and the lines between them matter when you are deciding what to buy.

  • CAD and BIM define the piece. Tekla Structures, Revit, ALLPLAN, and AutoCAD are the common tools.
  • MES gets the piece made. It works in hours and shifts, at the level of the station.
  • ERP runs the business around the piece: estimating, purchasing, inventory, dispatch, invoicing, and job cost.

When these are three products from three vendors, every handoff is a translation. The model is exported, converted for the MES, and keyed again into the ERP. Each handoff is a place for the schedule, the drawing, and the invoice to disagree.

Why Machinery Makers Sell Their Own MES

Companies that build precast machinery also sell software to run it. Progress Group sells ebos as its MES alongside erpbos, stabos, and Ahead APS. Elematic sells Plant Control, which includes FloorMES and WallMES, and ELiPLAN. Schnell Software sells Graphico Pro, Optimo, and Grafo Boss for the reinforcement shop.

That makes sense for the machinery maker. It also leaves many plants believing the software is part of the machine. It is not. The machine is hardware. The software layer on top of it is a separate decision.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a precast MES?

A precast manufacturing execution system (MES) is the software layer between the office and the production line. It takes element data from design, plans what gets cast where and when, sends work to the machines and crews, and records what was actually produced.

What is the difference between a precast MES and a precast ERP?

The MES gets the piece made at the level of the station and the shift. The ERP runs the business around it: estimating, purchasing, inventory, dispatch, invoicing, and job cost. CastLogic covers both roles in one platform.

Do I have to use the MES sold by my machinery manufacturer?

No. The machinery is hardware and the software layer is a separate choice. CastLogic replaces the software layer on Progress Group, Elematic, and Schnell machinery, and the hardware integrates into the CastLogic platform.

Can CastLogic work with the MES I already have?

Yes. CastLogic integrates with any MES and any machinery, so a plant can connect its existing MES first and replace it later.
