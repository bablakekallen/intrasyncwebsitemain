A Concrete Vision Alternative for Precast Producers: What to Compare

November 23, 2026 • 4 min read • By Zachary Frye

Concrete Vision has been part of precast plants for a long time, and plenty of producers know it well. If you run it today and are looking at what else is available, the useful comparison is not a feature count. It is where the edges of the system sit, and what you are running alongside it to cover the rest.

What Concrete Vision Covers

Concrete Vision describes itself as a web-based platform built for the precast concrete industry. Its site lists these standard modules:

  • Contact Management: the CRM, with activities, tasks, and contact search.
  • Estimating and Quoting: quote templates, takeoffs, bid tracking, and sales analytics.
  • Engineering Integration: imports piece and bill of material data from design tools.
  • Production Scheduling: drag-and-drop scheduling with bed layouts, labor, and material control.
  • Project Management: submittals, RFIs, change orders, and punch lists.
  • Quality Control: post-pour defects and non-conformance reports (NCRs).
  • Safety: near-miss and incident reporting with a form builder.
  • Yard Management: finished goods, raw material inventory, and storage plans.
  • Dispatch: load planning, ticketing, and delivery documentation.
  • Field: erection sequences, field NCRs, and daily reports.
  • Mobile: pre-pour and post-pour inspections, NCRs, yard updates, and trailer loading verification.

That is a broad operations footprint, from the first quote to the piece going up on site.

What Sits Outside It

The same published pages show where other systems come in:

  • Accounting: the module list does not include accounting, a general ledger, or invoicing, and the integrations page does not name an accounting package.
  • Structure design and quoting: Stack-It is listed as a sales integration for producing quotes and estimates.
  • QC testing and product tracking: ForneyVault and Idencia OneSource are listed as quality control integrations.
  • Detailing: Tekla Structures, Autodesk Revit, and ALLPLAN are listed as engineering integrations.
  • AI and machine vision: neither appears on the module, integration, or mobile pages.

None of that is a flaw on its own. Integrations are how most plants work. The question is how many separate systems, vendors, and renewals it adds up to, and where the numbers have to be keyed in twice.

  • Where does accounting live, and how does production data reach the general ledger?
